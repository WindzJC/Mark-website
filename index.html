01 The person behind the poems

A working man.
A writer's eye.

Born in Dallas and raised in Farmers Branch, Mark H. Brookshire's story isn't one of quiet rooms and perfect desks. It's a life lived in motion: on an oil rig, painting, in construction, and working as a cable technician.

He describes himself as no hero — simply someone who has worked, learned, and kept collecting thoughts along the way. A college poetry-contest win was an early signal. Writing stayed with him, even as his jobs changed.

That contrast is the point. The hands that did the work also found the words.

02 Dallas & the poem

A city.
A wound.
A voice.

Mark was five years old when President John F. Kennedy was assassinated in Dallas in 1963. A quarter-century later, he wrote a poem about the burden that tragedy left on the city and the people who called it home.

That poem, A Crime Revisited (1988), is documented in the collection of The Sixth Floor Museum at Dealey Plaza. A typed copy was displayed in a museum installation in 2005. In 2021, Mark recorded an oral history with the museum.

It is a rare thread connecting the poet's personal memory to a city's public history.

04 The collection

A book of rhymes & reflections.

Thrown Out of Eden: Rhymes of/and Reflection gathers Mark's observations and commentary into poetry. Its introduction insists that silence is no answer to difficult questions — and that the poet still has a role to play.

For readers drawn to language with rough edges, history in its bloodstream, and a voice that isn't afraid to be direct.

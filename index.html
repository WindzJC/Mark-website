01 The collection

Poetry with
something to say.

Thrown Out of Eden: Rhymes of/and Reflection is a collection shaped by a writer who believes poetry can do more than decorate a page. It can confront a difficult memory, question an easy assumption, or give a feeling its proper name.

The book moves between humanity, imagination, and social commentary—inviting readers to take their time, follow the reflections, and bring their own experiences to the words.

02 The poet

A working life.
A writer's eye.

Born in Dallas and raised in nearby Farmers Branch, Mark H. Brookshire followed a path that did not begin in a literary salon. His published biography recalls years spent in different lines of work—from an oil-rig deck to painting and construction, and work as a cable technician.

Through those experiences, writing remained part of his life. An early college poetry-contest win was one sign of a voice that would keep finding its way onto the page.

There is nothing ornamental about a life fully observed.

03 A poem in the archive

A Crime
Revisited.

Long before Thrown Out of Eden, Mark wrote about the city that raised him. His 1988 poem “A Crime Revisited” reflected on Dallas and the lasting shadow of President John F. Kennedy's assassination.

The poem now has a documented place in the collection of The Sixth Floor Museum at Dealey Plaza—a remarkable intersection of personal voice and public memory.
